BrlMultiline glyph vocabulary
=============================

Each symbol drawn beside the braille it stands in place of, on the same lines. The
glyph is on the left, then a < marker, then what NVDA puts on the display today.

The question is never whether a shape is a good drawing. It is whether the shape is
better under a finger than the cells it is taking the place of, which is a comparison
and needs both halves of it in view.

The dot numbering: 1, 2, 3 down the left column and 7 at its foot; 4, 5, 6 down the
middle and 8 at its foot -- braille's own. Then 9, 10, 11 down the third column, the
one braille leaves blank, and 12 at its foot. Twelve dots is one whole cell slot.

In the pictures, O is a raised pin and a full stop is a flat one. A glyph is three
columns to a cell; the braille it replaces is two, and that missing third column is
the gap the drawing reclaims -- it is why NVDA's checkbox has a hole through its
middle and the glyph does not.

The braille shown for a wording is the uncontracted alphabet. What actually reaches
the display is the reader's own table, asked for at the moment of use, and where a
contracted table makes a wording shorter than the shape the glyph is not drawn.
