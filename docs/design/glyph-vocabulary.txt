BrlMultiline glyph vocabulary
=============================

One entry per symbol. Each gives what NVDA writes today, what the glyph is meant to
look like, the dot numbers it is written in, and how many cells it gives back.

The dot numbering: 1, 2, 3 down the left column and 7 at its foot; 4, 5, 6 down the
middle and 8 at its foot -- braille's own. Then 9, 10, 11 down the third column, the
one braille leaves blank, and 12 at its foot. Twelve dots is one whole cell slot.

In the pictures, O is a raised pin and a full stop is a flat one. A glyph is three
columns wide; the braille it replaces is two, which is the gap the drawing reclaims.

To feel these rather than read them, assign "Graphics: Show the glyph catalogue"
in NVDA's Input Gestures under BrlMultiline. A routing key names the symbol under
your finger.
